About us

Healthcare is a right, not a privilege.

Free Healthcare Initiative of California is a youth-led nonprofit working to both educate and bring help to lower-income families. We want to make sure all individuals are getting the help they need, while also figuring out a way to give them the best care.

Too many people already qualify for coverage they never receive — not because they were turned away, but because no one ever explained it to them in language they could use. We exist to close that gap.

How we work

  • We translate. Guides and workshops that explain coverage without jargon.
  • We connect. Pointing people toward the free and low-cost care they're entitled to.
  • We advocate. Campaigning for billing rules patients can actually understand.

Where to turn, right now

You don't need to join anything, prove anything, or pay anything to use what's on this page.

Emergencies

  • Medical emergency: call 911. Emergency rooms must treat you regardless of your ability to pay.
  • Mental health or suicidal crisis: call or text 988. Free and confidential, 24 hours a day.

Free, unbiased counseling

  • Medicare questions — HICAP: 1-800-434-0222. State-funded California counselors who aren't selling plans, which makes them different from nearly everyone else offering Medicare help.
  • Medi-Cal (California's Medicaid): apply any time of year at coveredca.com. There's no enrollment deadline for Medi-Cal.
  • Covered California: subsidized private plans if you don't qualify for Medi-Cal. Same site.

Free and sliding-scale clinics

Federally funded health centers charge on a sliding scale based on income, and see patients regardless of insurance status.

Find a health center near you

Prescription costs

  • GoodRx and Cost Plus Drugs often beat insurance copays on generics. Worth checking before every fill.
  • Medicare Extra Help lowers Part D costs for people with limited income — apply through Social Security.

A bill that looks wrong

  1. Request a fully itemized bill. You have the right to one — always ask.
  2. Compare it against the Explanation of Benefits from your insurer.
  3. Ask the hospital about financial assistance or charity care. Nonprofit hospitals are required to offer it, and most patients never ask.
  4. Don't pay a disputed bill while it's under review.

This is exactly the problem our petition is trying to fix.

Medicare, explained plainly

By Sophie Tomooka and Krisha Mehta · Updated September 2026

Download as PDF

Medicare is the federal health insurance program for people aged 65 and older, along with people who have end-stage kidney failure or certain long-term disabilities. It exists so that people who have stopped working can still get care they could not otherwise pay for.

It comes in four parts, and they do different jobs.

PartWhat it coversWhat it costs
Part A
Hospital		 Inpatient hospital stays, surgeries, skilled nursing facility care, and hospice. Intensive care, not routine checkups or prescriptions. Usually no monthly premium — most people already paid for it through payroll taxes while working.
Part B
Medical		 Doctor visits, outpatient care, medical supplies, and preventive care including blood tests, X-rays, and flu shots. A monthly premium.
Part C
Medicare Advantage		 A private plan replacing Parts A and B. Often adds routine vision, hearing aids, or fitness benefits. Run by companies such as Aetna or UnitedHealthcare, not by the government. Varies by plan.
Part D
Prescriptions		 Optional drug coverage added to Original Medicare. Run by private companies under federal rules. Varies by plan, plus a deductible of up to $615 in 2026.

The $2,100 cap limits what you pay, not what the plan pays. In 2026, once your out-of-pocket spending on covered Part D drugs reaches $2,100 for the year, your plan covers 100% of your covered medications for the rest of the calendar year. Premiums don't count toward that total. This replaced the old "donut hole," which ended after 2024.

How to sign up

  1. Check whether you're eligible. Most people qualify at 65.
  2. Know your window. Your Initial Enrollment Period is seven months: the three months before your 65th birthday month, the birthday month itself, and the three months after. Missing it can mean a penalty added to your premium for life.
  3. Check whether you're already enrolled. If you receive Social Security or Railroad Retirement Board benefits, enrollment usually happens automatically.
  4. Apply. Online at ssa.gov is fastest. You can also call 1-800-772-1213 or visit a Social Security office.
  5. Start with Part A and Part B. Add Part D, or switch to Medicare Advantage, afterward.
  6. Watch for your card. Your Medicare number and card arrive by mail, and coverage usually begins the month you turn 65 if you enrolled early.

Nothing on this page is medical, legal, or insurance advice. For help with your own situation, call HICAP or a licensed counselor.

Our campaign

End hidden hospital charges

We're asking the San Diego County Board of Supervisors to support the Healthcare Billing Transparency Act — so every patient gets a bill they can actually read.

Sign the petition

The problem

Recent price-transparency laws require hospitals to post estimated prices and standard charges up front, so patients can plan ahead. But no law governs the bill that arrives after treatment. That bill can be vague, bundled, and unreadable — and nothing requires anyone to explain it to you.

Research published in the American Journal of Public Health found that medical problems and medical bills are a contributing factor in roughly two-thirds of U.S. personal bankruptcies. Billing errors are widely reported to affect a large share of hospital bills.

What we're proposing

The Healthcare Billing Transparency Act (HBTA) has two provisions:

  1. Automatic itemized billing. Every patient receives a full, line-by-line bill within five business days of treatment, with no request required. Bundled or vague charges are prohibited unless the patient waives itemization in writing.
  2. Plain-language service descriptions. Every billing code is paired with a clear, plain-English explanation, so patients understand what they're being charged for without outside research.

What we're asking for

That the San Diego County Board of Supervisors formally support the HBTA and advocate for its adoption at the state and federal level.
